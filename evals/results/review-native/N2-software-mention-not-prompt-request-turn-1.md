An **authorization boundary** is the limit of what someone has permission to do under the agreement made in a development discussion. It defines the approved actions, their scope, and the systems they may affect.

For example:

- **“Explain this bug”** authorizes investigation and explanation.
- **“Fix this bug”** usually authorizes changing the relevant code and testing the fix.
- **“Deploy the fix to staging”** authorizes that deployment; production deployment requires its own authorization.

Discussing an idea, recommending it, or agreeing that it would help does not automatically approve its implementation.

Within the approved scope, routine decisions can proceed without repeated permission checks. Crossing the boundary—for example, adding a paid service, deleting data, or deploying to production—requires authorization covering that action.

In a discussion, saying **“that crosses the authorization boundary”** means: **the proposed action goes beyond what has been agreed.**
